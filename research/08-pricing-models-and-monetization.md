# Pricing Models & Monetization Strategy

> How the global POS leaders make money, what to steal, and how to build the best monetization model for African markets.

---

## Global POS Pricing Models — Comparison

### Model 1: Software Subscription + Payment Processing (Toast, Lightspeed)
- Monthly software fee per location
- Mandatory payment processing at fixed rates
- **Revenue per merchant**: $100-400/month software + $500-3,000/month in payment fees
- **Lock-in mechanism**: Hardware is proprietary. Switching requires new hardware.
- **Risk**: Merchants resent payment lock-in if rates aren't competitive

### Model 2: Freemium + Payment Processing (Square)
- Free POS software
- Revenue entirely from payment processing (2.6% + $0.10)
- **Revenue per merchant**: $0 software + ~$1,000-5,000/month in payment fees at volume
- **Lock-in mechanism**: Data, integrations, embedded banking
- **Risk**: Requires payment volume to be profitable

### Model 3: Hardware Bundle + Processing (SkyTab/Shift4, Clover)
- Free or subsidized hardware in exchange for processing commitment
- Hardware costs amortized over 3-5 year contract
- **Revenue per merchant**: Processing fee + software fee
- **Lock-in mechanism**: Hardware contract with ETF (early termination fee)
- **Risk**: Hardware economics require volume

### Model 4: Enterprise License + Custom (Oracle MICROS, NCR)
- Annual license per location
- Implementation fees
- Support contracts
- Custom hardware
- **Revenue per merchant**: $5,000-50,000/year
- **Lock-in mechanism**: Switching costs massive (data migration, retraining, implementation)

### Model 5: Per-Register (Revel, ConnectPOS)
- $99-199/register/month
- Works well for multi-terminal businesses
- **Revenue per merchant**: Scales with size of operation

### Model 6: One-Time License (Magestore)
- Buy once, own forever
- **Revenue**: Upfront license + annual support (optional)
- **Risk**: Less predictable recurring revenue
- **Opportunity**: Lower resistance to purchase

## The Stacking Revenue Model (Best Practice)

The most profitable POS companies stack multiple revenue streams:

```
Layer 1: Software Subscription      → $20-150/month
Layer 2: Payment Processing         → 0.5-2.5% of GMV
Layer 3: Financial Products         → Interest on loans, float income
Layer 4: Hardware                   → One-time sale or lease
Layer 5: Marketplace/App Revenue    → Rev share from 3rd party apps
Layer 6: Premium Features           → Add-ons at extra cost
Layer 7: Data/Insights              → B2B data products
```

Toast does all 7 layers. Square does 1, 2, 3, 5, 6. That's why Toast has higher ARPU.

## Pricing Strategies — What to Steal

### Strategy 1: Free Tier That Actually Works (Square)
**What**: Free POS with real functionality. No time limit.
**Why it works**: Reduces sales friction to zero. Merchant starts, grows, eventually needs premium features. Zero cost to acquire.
**How to implement**: Free plan = core POS + mobile money + up to 100 products + 1 location + basic reports.

### Strategy 2: Hardware as Customer Acquisition (SkyTab)
**What**: Free hardware if you process with us.
**Why it works**: Hardware cost is the biggest objection. Remove it. Recover cost through processing fees over 24 months.
**How to implement**: Partner with a hardware manufacturer. Give hardware free on 12-month contract at your processing rates. Recover cost in months 2-12.

### Strategy 3: Embedded Lending (Toast Capital)
**What**: Offer business loans based on POS transaction history.
**Why it works**: Extremely high margins (15-25% effective interest), zero-friction for merchant (it just deducts from daily sales), builds deep loyalty.
**How to implement**: Partner with a licensed lender or get lending license. Use 6 months of POS transaction history as underwriting. Offer advances up to 30% of monthly GMV. Repay as 5-15% of daily sales.

### Strategy 4: Surcharging (Shift4)
**What**: Merchant passes card processing fee to customer. Cash price vs. card price.
**Why it works**: Makes processing cost-neutral for merchant. Very appealing to low-margin businesses.
**How to implement**: Dual pricing display — show cash price and card price separately. Legal in most African jurisdictions (customers often already pay this informally).

## African Market Pricing Strategy Recommendation

### The Right Approach for Africa

**Phase 1: Market Entry (Year 1-2)**
| Plan | Monthly | Includes |
|------|---------|---------|
| Starter | Free | Core POS, M-Pesa, up to 50 products, 1 terminal, basic reports |
| Business | KSh 1,999 (~$15) | Unlimited products, inventory, customers, loyalty, WhatsApp receipts |
| Pro | KSh 4,999 (~$38) | Multi-location, advanced analytics, AI features, API access |
| Enterprise | Custom | White-label, franchise management, custom integrations |

**Payment Processing Revenue**
- Partner with Safaricom/MPESA for merchant rate share
- Negotiate bulk rates with payment processors
- Share 0.5-1% of GMV processed through the platform
- For mobile money: negotiate 0.3-0.5% revenue share on merchant transactions

**Hardware Strategy**
- Sell affordable Android tablets + receipt printer + cash drawer as starter kit
- Price: ~$200-300 for full kit (vs. Toast's $800+)
- No proprietary hardware — use standard Android
- Offer monthly hardware rental: ~KSh 500/month for the kit

**Financial Services (Year 2+)**
- Partner with a local bank or SACCO for merchant loans
- Use 3+ months of POS history as the credit model
- Target advance: 1-2× monthly GMV
- Revenue: fee-sharing arrangement with lending partner

### Unit Economics Target

| Metric | Target |
|--------|--------|
| Monthly software ARPU | $15-30 |
| Monthly payments revenue per merchant | $20-100 |
| Monthly financial services per merchant | $50-200 (for borrowing merchants) |
| Blended ARPU target | $35-80/month |
| Customer acquisition cost | <$50 |
| Payback period | <6 months |
| Gross margin on software | 80%+ |
| Gross margin on payments | 30-40% |

## What NOT to Do (Learn from Failures)

### Don't: Payment Lock-In at Non-Competitive Rates
Toast forces payment processing at 2.49% — non-negotiable. African merchants are extremely price-sensitive. If your rates aren't competitive, you'll lose to mobile money (which is 0.5-1%). Be competitive or be agnostic.

### Don't: Long-Term Contracts at Launch
African merchants are skeptical of technology companies. Month-to-month wins trust. Earn the right to ask for a longer commitment after 6-12 months.

### Don't: Only USD Pricing
Price in local currency. KSh, NGN, UGX, TZS. Don't make merchants do mental math every time they see your price. It signals that you understand their market.

### Don't: Charge for Essential Integrations
M-Pesa, WhatsApp, basic accounting — these should be included. Only charge for truly premium features.
